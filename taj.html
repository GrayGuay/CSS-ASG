About the Taj Mahal

The Taj Mahal is a magnificent white marble mausoleum located in Agra, India. Built by the Mughal Emperor Shah Jahan, it was constructed in memory of his beloved wife Mumtaz Mahal. The Taj Mahal is renowned for its stunning symmetry, intricate carvings, and beautiful gardens. It is recognized as a UNESCO World Heritage Site and is considered one of the New Seven Wonders of the World.

History

Construction of the Taj Mahal began in 1632 and was completed in 1653, employing thousands of artisans and craftsmen. The complex includes a mosque, guest house, gardens, and reflecting pools, all designed in the traditional Mughal style. Over the centuries, the Taj Mahal has faced threats from pollution and natural aging, leading to ongoing preservation efforts. Today, it stands as a global symbol of love, architectural excellence, and India’s rich cultural heritage. Learn more about the Mughal Empire.

Timeline

1632 – Foundation Laid

Emperor Shah Jahan ordered the construction of the Taj Mahal to honor his beloved wife Mumtaz Mahal.

1632–1643 – Main Mausoleum Construction

The central white marble tomb was constructed, with thousands of artisans, craftsmen, and laborers involved.

1644–1653 – Completion of the Complex

The gardens, mosque, guest house, and other structures were completed, finishing the iconic Taj Mahal complex.

20th–21st Century – Preservation Efforts

Restoration projects were undertaken to protect the Taj Mahal from pollution, aging, and environmental damage.

Culture

The Taj Mahal is a symbol of eternal love, built by Emperor Shah Jahan for his wife Mumtaz Mahal. Beyond its romantic association, the mausoleum reflects Mughal architectural brilliance, combining Persian, Islamic, and Indian design elements.

The complex also served religious purposes, with the mosque on its western side used for daily prayers. Its gardens and symmetrical layout reflect the Islamic concept of paradise.

Fun Facts

Colour Change

The Taj Mahal changes colour depending on sunlight — appearing pink in the morning, white during the day, and golden under moonlight.

Architectural Design

The central marble dome stands 73 metres tall and is surrounded by four smaller domes, creating perfect symmetry.

Workforce

Over 20,000 artisans, craftsmen, and labourers were involved in building the entire Taj Mahal complex.
